## 1. What changed mathematically

The old endpoint was a transfer criterion for odd exponent N, with an explicit conductor-15 finite-factor treatment. The new proposed endpoint has **no parity restriction** and does not enumerate exceptional conductors.

For any N >= 2, take every full ordered branch family of connected abelian covers of P1 whose deck-group exponent divides N. The proposed result gives an algebraic-correspondence presentation of the connected-monodromy invariants on every power of its covering Jacobian by Fermat determinant summands, first cohomology of Fermat-Jacobian factors, and Tate objects.

It follows that all-powers rational HC for the degree-N Fermat Jacobian is equivalent to all-powers rational HC for the very general Jacobians in all these families. **The forward direction is the substantive assertion.** The reverse merely includes the Fermat curve as a three-branch (Z/N)^2 cover. It is not an independent difficult reduction.

There is also an unconditional Hodge-theoretic formulation, relative to the written proof: the rigid tensor category generated by all these monodromy-invariant Hodge structures and Tate objects is exactly the category generated by H1 of the Fermat curve and Tate objects. This is not a Chow-motive equivalence or a claim of an algebraic splitting of every Hodge surjection.

The all-powers premise at arbitrary N remains a premise. Aoki supplies it for prime powers and twice prime powers, and for N=2^a 3^b 5^c 7^d with c=0 or d=0. Thus the new proposed corollaries include all abelian 2-groups and these even mixed-exponent groups. No general Fermat theorem outside the established list is claimed.

## 2. Proof changes, with attribution

### High rank: a previously published argument, not a new parity trick

In the proof of Menet--Nguyen Proposition 6.2, pair-generator spectra first force **linear** conjugacy from projective conjugacy when the eigenspace dimension is at least three. Triple Dehn-twist traces then reconstruct individual local roots. This does not divide by two and already works at even conductor.

The new note applies that argument to two arbitrary character words on the same support. It does not claim invention of the method. Comparing isolated projective triple spectra would not be an adequate substitute for the simultaneous linear normalization.

### Rank two: exclude the extra uniform half-turn

For four active roots (u,v,w,(uvw)^(-1)), the adjacent braid matrices derived from the published Gram formulas are

    A = [[u*v, v*(1-u)], [0, 1]]
    B = [[1, 0], [1-w, v*w]].

Their scalar-conjugacy invariant is

    J = tr(A) tr(B) tr(AB) / (det(A) det(B))
      = (1+u*v)(1+u*w)(1+v*w)/(u*v*w)
      = sum_i (q_i + q_i^(-1)).

The pair spectra leave, besides the already known ±Klein-four operations, only the uniform half-turn q_i -> -q_i. It changes J to -J. When J=0, a pair and its complement both have product -1, and the half-turn is already inversion followed by a double transposition. Therefore no new even-conductor graph survives.

The modulus-eight words (1,1,1,5) and (5,5,5,1) are a useful falsifier for pair-only reasoning: their pair spectra agree, but their J values are opposite and nonzero, so their moving factors are independent.

This is an application of classical trace algebra. Exact priority for its use in this uniform character-word classification is not established. The note does not call the trace invariant itself new.

### Constant factors: a uniform three-point isogeny, not a new finite classification

For a primitive cyclic word b at conductor d and support s, let

    Q_u = sum_i [u*b_i]_d / d.

A mixed embedding gives full complex SL_(s-2) closure via Menet--Nguyen and Galois transport. Otherwise all Q_u are 1 or s-1. In that all-definite case put

    e = gcd(d,b_1,b_2), m=d/e,
    beta = (b_1/e,b_2/e,-(b_1+b_2)/e) mod m.

This is an active connected three-point word. Its primitive rank-one Hodge structure has exactly the original pure type at each embedding. With h=s-2, the entire original primitive factor is isogenous to

    B_beta ^ (h * [Q(zeta_d):Q(zeta_m)]).

The isogeny follows from an explicit K_d-linear Hodge comparison and full faithfulness in weight one. B_beta is a factor of the Fermat-m Jacobian, hence of the Fermat-N source when d divides N. This handles every all-definite factor, including even conductor and the old conductor-15 exception, without degeneration or a finite case list.

**Ghate--Venkataramana already classify finite primitive monodromy.** That discovery must not be presented as ours. The new note's contribution here is the short uniform three-point algebraic model, whose exact historical priority may itself be classical.

### Assembly: what remains classical, and what must still be reviewed

Schoen's primitive determinant construction works at composite degree; the repository's finite quotient normalization has no oddness dependency. Shioda--Katsura controls the requisite blowup centers and reduces products of Fermat hypersurfaces to powers of the Fermat curve. Aoki supplies established all-powers cycle inputs.

The proposed simultaneous classification, now including independent quadratic symplectic factors, feeds the existing full-rational-source invariant-theory argument. **Full rational tensors** are essential: mixed-conductor sources need not have rank one over a common cyclotomic field. All-definite factors require their whole first cohomology, not just determinants. Semisimplicity supplies a Hodge lift; algebraicity is required only for the forward correspondence maps, not for that splitting.

None of these steps uses factorwise HC as a substitute for mixed classes, identifies the full monodromy commutant with Hodge endomorphisms, or replaces the full branch configuration by an arbitrary subfamily.

## 4. Reproducible checks and exact limits

Run from the repository root with Python >=3.10 and SymPy (the recorded run used Python 3.13.5 and SymPy 1.14.0):

```sh
python scripts/check_all_exponent.py --max-modulus 32 \
  --output output/certificates/all_exponent_report.json
python -O scripts/check_all_exponent.py --max-modulus 32 \
  --output /tmp/all_exponent_optimized.json
```

The default report records five symbolic identities; 236,096 active zero-sum four-words at moduli 2..32; 46,032 nongraph half-turn candidates separated; 47 pair-kernel solutions; and 391 normalized primitive all-definite four-words through conductor 32, including 36 with a proper triangle conductor. It also records the explicit even collision and three higher/contextual finite-factor examples.

The four-word comparisons use one representative per pair-spectrum bucket, not an unstated exhaustive count of all ordered pairs of words. The definite-word enumeration normalizes the first residue sum to d; simultaneous negation covers the sum 3d. It does not claim exhaustive higher-support testing. The script uses explicit exceptions rather than `assert`, so Python optimization does not erase the checks.

The manuscript was compiled to a nine-page PDF and its rendered pages inspected. No repository-wide Lean build, dependency audit, or original certificate replay was performed in this continuation environment. GitHub reads and writes were available, but the container could not obtain a full checkout. No claim of green full-project CI is made. The PDF is a local review artifact; its source is committed, and it is not silently added to the old PDF/source hash manifest.

The result is stronger and more coherent than appending another prime-specific corollary: the exponent restriction is removed from the reduction itself, and the moving and constant contributions are handled uniformly. Nevertheless the ingredients are substantially classical, the remaining new proofs are short, and breadth is not a substitute for depth or priority. A submission-level assessment requires specialist review of the exact group theorem and algebraic assembly, followed by a full prior-theorem comparison. The target journals should not be promised on the basis of this draft or its finite regressions.
