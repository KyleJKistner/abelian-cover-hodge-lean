## Frozen scope

The theorem concerns the very general Jacobian in a full family of connected
\((\mathbf Z/p\mathbf Z)^m\)-covers of \(\mathbf P^1\), with \(p\) an odd prime. It determines the full connected generic Hodge group, including its central torus, classifies every generic Hodge tensor on every power, constructs algebraic cycles representing all determinant/Weil tensors, and proves the rational Hodge conjecture on every power. The binary case is handled separately by the symplectic Phase I argument.

The package does not claim results for composite exponent, special-fiber Mumford-Tate jumps, or nonabelian deck groups.

## Headline theorem

For a full-support branch code
\[
C\subset \mathbf F_p^r\cap \mathbf 1^\perp,
\]
let \(P_C\) be the saturated centralizer from Phase I, \(D_C=P_C/P_C^{\rm der}\), and let \(\Sigma_C\) be the signature map on the oriented determinant lattice. Then
\[
\operatorname{Hg}(A)=\pi^{-1}(T_C),\qquad
X^*(T_C)\cong \operatorname{im}\Sigma_C,
\]
and the full generic Mumford-Tate group is \(\mathbf G_m\operatorname{Hg}(A)\). Every Hodge tensor is generated by contractions and determinant words in \(\ker\Sigma_C\); every such determinant word is represented by a compact-type fusion cycle. Consequently every rational Hodge class on every power of the very general Jacobian is algebraic.

## Exact certificate

`phase_II_certificate.py` performs the following independent checks:

1. constructs the integer signature matrix from a full-support code;
2. computes a certified Smith-normal-form \(\mathbf Z\)-basis of each sampled integral relation lattice;
3. verifies that each basis relation gives the required balanced residue sums;
4. verifies opposite multiplicities and the pairing-graph decomposition;
5. checks the compact-type fusion rank identity on every graph component;
6. verifies the mixed \(p=5\) relation and its fused six-point tuple;
7. exhausts the zero-signature disk criterion for normalized disks for all primes through 19;
8. verifies vanishing of the signature torus for the split elementary-\(p\) family for every odd prime through 31;
9. records hashes of the final Phase I and Phase II sources.

The certificate is regression evidence for the finite arithmetic interfaces. It does not replace Aoki's theorem, the Phase I monodromy proof, invariant theory, admissible-cover deformation theory, Schoen's cycle theorem, or Chow specialization.

## Principal specialist-review targets

The theorem stack is complete, but the following are the most valuable points for independent review:

1. **Determinant quotient and rational descent.** Reconstruct Proposition 3.2 from the complex block decomposition and verify that the Kummer relation lattice is exactly the kernel on the connected determinant quotient.
2. **Full Hodge group.** Verify the use of the Galois-span criterion and the passage from the derived group plus projected Hodge circle to \(\operatorname{Hg}(A)=\pi^{-1}(T_C)\).
3. **Fusion deformation.** Check that the glued inverse-inertia nodes define the stated balanced twisted \(\mathbf Z/p\)-cover and that simultaneous smoothing has the claimed simple branch tuple.
4. **Specialization of Schoen cycles.** Verify the \(K\)-linear determinant identification and compatibility of cycle specialization with the compact-type Jacobian decomposition.
5. **Tannakian generation.** Check that contractions and determinant tensors exhaust the invariants for every standard/dual multiplicity pattern and that the rational descent is exact.

## Exact boundary

The proof uses prime exponent twice in an essential way:

- every nonzero inertia element has full order, so the fused admissible cover has one node above each target node and remains compact type;
- Aoki's theorem turns every balanced determinant word into opposite pairs.

For composite exponent, balanced non-simple words occur and the present fusion/Schoen argument does not automatically algebraize every relation. That is outside the theorem rather than an unresolved lemma inside it.
